# **🎮 Dev Log #1 – The Journey of Creating**

# **Maze Escape**

## **👋 Hello! I’m making a game**

Hi there 👋

I’m currently on a journey to **build a small game called Maze Escape on my own**.

This is not a high-end or complex game.

Instead, it is a **simple but fun** experience where players:

- 🧭 Navigate through a **maze**
- 😈 Avoid **enemies**
- 💰 Find the **treasure**
- 🏆 And look for a way **out**

👉 Most importantly, **I am building this game from scratch**, and I want to share the entire journey along the way.

---

## **❓ Why did I make Maze Escape?**

The reason is very simple:

- I **like games**
- I **want to learn game development**
- And I truly believe that:
    
    > Anyone can make a game, even students
    > 

Maze Escape is designed to be:

- Easy to play
- Easy to understand
- And easy to learn from

If you have ever thought:

> “I like games, but I don’t know where to start…”
> 

👉 Then this dev log is for you.

---

## **🛠️ What tools am I using?**

I am currently using:

- 🎮 **Godot Engine 4.5** (free and very powerful)
- 🧠 **GDScript** for coding (Python-like and easy to read)
- 🧩 A **top-down** game style

You don’t need a high-end computer.

You don’t need to know advanced programming from the beginning.

👉 You only need **patience and curiosity**.

---

## **🧱 What does the first version include?**

In the first prototype, I have implemented:

### **✅ Player**

- Movement inside the maze
- Collision detection (no walking through walls)

### **✅ Enemies**

- Automatic back-and-forth movement
- Contact results in **game over**

### **✅ Treasure**

- Touching it means **victory**

It sounds simple, right?

However, each feature taught me **many new concepts**.

---

## **🤯 What was the hardest part at the beginning?**

Honestly… **there were many bugs** 😅

- The player did not move
- Collisions did not trigger a loss
- The game worked on my computer but failed on the web
- Mobile behavior was different from desktop

But every time I fixed one issue, I felt:

> “Wow, I’m really making a game.”
> 

---

## **🌱 The most important lesson I learned**

👉 Making a game is not about:

- Writing perfect code from day one ❌
- Or creating a flawless game ❌

👉 It is about:

- Taking **small steps**
- Understanding why something breaks
- And **not giving up**
