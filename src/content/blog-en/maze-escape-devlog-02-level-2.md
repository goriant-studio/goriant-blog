## **🧱 Devlog – Level 2 Takes Shape, Enemy Standardized & First Tiles Born 🎉**

Today I continued building **Level 2 for MazeEscape**, and honestly… it felt really good 😄😄😄

**Everything is finally starting to take proper shape.**

---

### **🎯 Achievements unlocked today**

- ✅ Refactored **Enemy into an abstract, data-driven design**
- ✅ Only **one Enemy type left** — different levels just change stats & sprites
- ✅ Much cleaner scenes, the editor layout makes sense at a glance
- ✅ Practiced **TileSet + TileMap** (not professional auto-tiling yet 😅)
- ✅ **Finished drawing the Level 2 maze using TileMap**
- ✅ And most importantly…

👉 **Used Affinity Studio for the first time to draw my own 64×64 game assets** 🎨✨

---

### **🧠 What I learned today**

Before this, I usually:

- focused on making things work first
- spawned nodes directly in scripts for speed

But once I moved into Level 2, it became clear that:

- Scenes should **explain their own structure**
- Logic should be separated from data
- Enemies shouldn’t know which level they’re in

Now:

- Enemies only need a sprite + a few numbers
- Level 1 / Level 2 mainly differ in **layout and gameplay pacing**

It feels very… “by the book” — in a good way.

---

### **🧩 About TileMap & TileSet**

I’m not doing professional auto-tiling yet (still learning 😄)

but:

- I now understand how TileMap works
- I manually **built the Level 2 maze using tiles**
- and most importantly: **the game runs and is playable**

👉 For me, that’s more than enough to keep going.

---

### **🎨 First 64×64 game asset**

A small milestone, but a very happy one:

- First time opening **Affinity Studio**
- First time setting a 64×64 canvas
- First time drawing tiles for my own game

It’s not amazing visually, but:

👉 **It’s mine**, and it’s running inside the game 😄

MazeEscape is starting to **feel like a real game**.
